# Gun-related homicides in Texas — verified stats

**Date:** 2026-11-08
**Status:** Complete — all three universes verified from primary sources (DPS, FBI CDE, CDC WONDER).
**Scope:** statewide Texas, 2020–2025, plus the six largest cities. "Gun-related homicide" = a homicide in which a firearm was the weapon. Three different universes measure it — they are reported separately and never merged.

## The three universes (read this before quoting any number)

1. **Texas DPS *Crime in Texas* (UCR/NIBRS, police-reported offenses).** The state's official series, from 1,600+ agencies. Counts *offenses*. 2025 data includes only reports received by **March 20, 2026**; the UCR collection is live and agencies can revise. NIBRS reporting became mandatory in Texas in September 2023, which changed coverage (small agencies joined; volume up, clearances down in the under-2,500 population range).
2. **FBI Crime Data Explorer (CDE/SHR, police-reported).** Same underlying police reports, FBI's cut. Annual totals run a few cases above DPS (2024: CDE 1,656 vs DPS 1,645) — revision timing and coverage, not an error. The SHR adds weapon, circumstance, relationship, and demographics per homicide.
3. **CDC WONDER (death certificates, ICD-10 X93–X95 assault by firearm).** Counts *deaths*, not police offenses. The authoritative firearm-homicide death count. It will differ from police counts (different case definitions, timing, and inclusion rules). **Numbers pending — see below.**

Never add a DPS number to a WONDER number, or average them. They answer different questions.

## 2. The gun share — DPS Weapons Section (2025)

*Source: DPS 2025, Section 5, "Firearms Used by Violent Crime Offense." "Firearm" includes all gun types: Firearm, Handgun, Rifle, Shotgun, Other Firearm.*

| Offense | Firearm-involved | Total with a weapon recorded | % with firearm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Murder & Nonnegligent Manslaughter | **1,025** | 1,391 | **73.69%** |
| Aggravated Assault | 23,764 | 66,031 | 35.99% |
| Robbery | 7,095 | 17,019 | 41.69% |
| Rape | 236 | 15,001 | 1.57% |
| **All violent crime** | **32,126** | 99,770 | **32.20%** |

Read it precisely: **1,025 murders involved a firearm** in 2025. The 73.69% is the share *among the 1,391 murders with a weapon type recorded*; 67 murders have no weapon recorded, so the firearm share of *all* murders is 70.3% (1,025/1,458). Firearms are far more concentrated in murder than in violent crime overall (73.7% vs 32.2%).

Note the gap vs DPS 2025 (73.7%): different years (2020–24 vs 2025), different universes (FBI SHR vs Texas UCR), and different weapon-recording rates. Both are correct inside their own universe; the DPS 2025 figure is the current single-year number, the SHR figure is the five-year FBI picture.

### Circumstances — the expressive / instrumental signal

| Circumstance (SHR) | Count | Share of known |
|---|---|---|
| Undetermined (facts do not permit determination) | 3,875 | — (40.0% of all) |
| Other Arguments | 2,947 | 50.7% |
| Other | 1,853 | 31.9% |
| Narcotic Drug Laws | 398 | 6.9% |
| Robbery | 186 | 3.2% |
| Gangland Killings | 119 | 2.0% |
| Motor Vehicle Theft | 73 | 1.3% |

| Burglary / Larceny / Arson / Rape | 78 | 1.3% |
| Juvenile Gang Killings | 12 | 0.2% |
| Domestic Violence | 11 | 0.2% |

Known circumstances: 5,808 (60.0%). **Among known circumstances, arguments are half (50.7%) — the expressive signal dominates Texas homicides, exactly as the national SHR pattern shows.** Felony-type circumstances (robbery, burglary, larceny, motor-vehicle theft, arson, rape) total 337, **5.8% of known** — the instrumental share. Gang (gangland + juvenile gang) is 131, 2.3%. "Other" (1,853, 31.9% of known) is uninformative — it is a residual category, not a finding.

Caveat: 40% undetermined is the largest single bucket. Any claim about "why" Texas homicides happen rests on the 60% with a recorded circumstance, and the undetermined 40% could differ systematically.

### Victims and offenders (SHR, 2020–2024)

- **Victims:** male 7,470 (77.1%), female 2,180 (22.5%). Age 20–29: 2,710 (28.0%); 30–39: 2,267 (23.4%); 10–19: 1,310 (13.5%). Race: White 5,467 (56.5%), Black or African American 3,955 (40.8%), Asian 141. Ethnicity: Hispanic or Latino 3,464 (35.8%), Not Hispanic or Latino 5,732 (59.2%). (Race and ethnicity overlap in SHR — do not add them.)
- **Offenders:** male 10,119, female 1,312, unknown 1,032. Age 20–29: 4,085; 10–19: 2,604. Offender counts exceed victim counts (multiple offenders per homicide).
- **Relationship:** unknown 5,582 (57.7%) — the largest bucket. Largest known: stranger 3,003, acquaintance 2,415, friend 353, girlfriend 344, wife 309. Relationship counts are per victim–offender pair and can exceed victim counts; treat them as counts, not victim shares.

## 5. The six largest cities — homicides (FBI CDE, agency-reported)

*Source: FBI CDE API, `/summarized/agency/{ORI}/homicide`, monthly actuals summed by year. These are **all-weapon homicides** — the agency endpoint does not break out weapon, so there is no verified firearm-only count per city yet.*

| City (PD) | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2020–24 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Houston | 415 | 475 | 437 | 354 | 334 | 2,015 |
| Dallas | 247 | 210 | 212 | 244 | 181 | 1,094 |
| San Antonio | 131 | 164 | 233 | 163 | 126 | 817 |
| Fort Worth | 109 | 118 | 101 | 85 | 72 | 485 |
| Austin | 45 | 81 | 64 | 64 | 66 | 320 |
| El Paso | 23 | 33 | 22 | 37 | 20 | 135 |
| **Six-city total** | **970** | **1,081** | **1,069** | **907** | **799** | **4,866** |

- Houston is 20.2% of the statewide 2024 total (334/1,656). The six cities together are 48.2% of 2024 homicides (799/1,656) and 50.2% over 2020–24.
- San Antonio peaked in 2022 (233) and has fallen since; Austin's 2021 spike (81) is the outlier in its series. Houston fell every year after 2021 (475 → 334, −29.7%).
- For reference, DPS 2024 murder rates per 100,000 (secondary citation, *Crime in Texas* 2024 via press summary — not yet verified against the 2024 PDF): Houston 13.8, Dallas 13.61, San Antonio 8.41, Austin 6.6. Do not publish the rates without that check.
- Do NOT apply the statewide firearm share to a city to estimate "gun homicides" there — that would be an estimate dressed as a measurement. City firearm counts need city incident data (Houston PD open data exists; Dallas also publishes open crime data — see §7).

95% CIs (where WONDER showed them): 2016: 4.1–4.6; 2017: 4.2–4.7; 2018: 3.8–4.2; 2019: 4.2–4.7; 2020: 5.6–6.2; 2021: 6.3–6.9; 2022: 5.8–6.4; 2023: 5.3–5.9; 2024: 4.4–4.9.

**How it compares with the police counts.** The death-certificate count runs *above* the police firearm count for the same years: WONDER 2020–2024 firearm deaths total **8,658**, vs SHR firearm homicides **7,480** over the same span. That gap is the known pattern — about 1,000 SHR homicides have no weapon recorded, and SHR coverage is incomplete — not a contradiction. The peak year agrees across all three universes (2021), and the decline since is consistent: WONDER firearm deaths fell 25.3% from the 2021 peak to 2024 (1,942 → 1,450); DPS murders fell 29.6% from 2021 to 2025. WONDER 2024 (1,450 firearm deaths) sits below DPS 2024 total murders (1,645, all weapons), as it must — firearm deaths are a subset of all murders.

**What WONDER cannot do:** no circumstance, no relationship, no weapon subtype beyond X93/X94/X95, and no sub-state geography through the API (this query used the web UI). For county or city firearm deaths, the route is CDC NVSS mortality files or the WONDER web UI with a county grouping.

## Secondary-source error caught

A viral infographic attributed to "Texas DPS, 2025 Crime in Texas" claims murder fell **31.62%** in 2025. That is wrong: −31.62% is the 2024→25 change for **Human Trafficking — Commercial Sex Acts** (427 → 292). Murder fell **11.37%** in volume. Do not repeat the infographic's figure.
